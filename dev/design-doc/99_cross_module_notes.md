## Cross-Module Notes (running list, not yet assigned to a module)
- **Escalation/"Great Wars" pathway**: no global tension metric. Emergent from accumulated bilateral relationship failures (broken treaties, forced annexations, ignored claims, cataclysm use) filtered through population will (Nations, Internal Politics). No module "owns" this — it's a designed emergent property of Countries + Nations + Diplomacy + Internal Politics acting together. Worth revisiting once those modules are drafted to make sure the incentive structure actually produces this behavior.
- Terrain/resource data is shared between World Map and Economics — needs one owner (World Map) with Economics reading, not duplicating.
- **Reputation-like values (trust, prestige-equivalents) may be cached/stored for performance**, but must always be re-derivable from the underlying material/event history — never a freely-spent independent currency.
- **Meteomancy** = the world's magical tradition as a whole, not weather magic specifically. Named for its method — practitioners work by observing and manipulating sky phenomena and the aether — but its effects can, in principle, do anything "common magic" could do in a more conventional fantasy setting. The defining constraint is *how* it works, not *what* it can achieve: it's slow, ritual-based, and reagent-consuming (Magical Reagents → Processed Reagents → ritual action) rather than instant spellcasting. Performed at population scale by common meteomants (Economics/Internal Politics territory). Immediate/personal magic exists only for rare named Archmages (Characters module concern) — the vast majority of the world's magical output is the slow, industrial, ritual kind.
- **Skilled labor as a distinct economic input** (separate from raw materials): population carries trained capacity across Agriculture, Extraction, Crafting/Industry, Ritual, Culture, Medicine, Builders, Diplomacy, Espionage, Teaching, and the five Army sub-categories (Shock Infantry, Skirmishers, Cavalry, Artillery, Siege Corps). Slow-moving stat; Teaching replenishes all categories including itself, so neglecting it causes visible long-term de-skilling. Economics owns the stat and the production/War-composition chains it feeds. Administration draws on a separately-tracked domestic trio — Bureaucrats, Army (administrative), Secret Police — for Control/Coordination/Observation; Diplomacy draws on its own dedicated Diplomacy/Espionage labor for external action, deliberately **not** a projection or export of domestic administrative capacity — a country can be domestically excellent and externally inept, or vice versa, depending on where it has actually invested.
- **Faction rebellion and Nations' Statehood Pathway are the same underlying event, viewed through two modules' lenses** — confirmed during the Administration discussion. When a rebelling Administration Faction and a majority-population Nation-in-territory coincide, it reads as a Nations-driven independence bid; when the rebelling Faction is cross-cultural, it reads as a pure Countries/Administration Formation-via-secession with no Nations angle. No separate reconciliation logic is needed — just consistent trigger conditions read from both sides.
- **Characters is a live, if deferred, dependency of both Administration and Diplomacy**: Faction leadership (specific nobles, specific bishops, a Secret Police chief) and individual envoys/spymasters are explicitly where each module's formulas will eventually need Characters' data — flagged now so the future Characters module design doesn't have to retrofit this.
- **Shared skin/skeleton pattern between Administration and Diplomacy**: both modules resolve proposals through the same underlying shape — a party's stance (material self-interest × modifier) weighed against accumulated relationship history, resolved via Persuasion/Control-equivalent mechanics — but present entirely different vocabularies to the player (Loyalty/Faction/Question domestically; Opinion/Leverage/Goals externally). Diplomatic ratification is gated by Administration's Veto/Loyalty machinery underneath, but this is never surfaced directly; a player who works out the connection independently is meant to be rewarded with better play, not taught it through UI.
